How We Work

Ductless Mini Split Installation, Done Properly

Design is where mini split projects succeed or fail, and Yakima HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because an oversized head short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and hunts. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, hidden where they can be and tidy where they cannot.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a measured, micron-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since a weeping flare is the classic mini split failure. Condensate is routed to drain by gravity or a reliable pump, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings actually explained. Done Right

The Benefits of Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation

Most failed mini splits we see in Yakima come from installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head blows warm. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves contamination inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs steal capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will drip down the wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Yakima HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and commissioning each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a place to improvise. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Homeowner Tips

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Get a yearly professional checkup of charge, flare joints, and drainage while any fix is still small
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Resist turning the system off and on; an inverter is most efficient holding one steady temperature
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
